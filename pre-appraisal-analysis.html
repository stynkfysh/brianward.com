Pre-Appraisal Analysis

Not an appraisal or an appraiser's opinion of value — and priced accordingly.

Before you order an appraisal — or instead of one, if all you need is clarity — the Pre-Appraisal Analysis shows you exactly what the market around your property has actually done. It is built from the same data sources my appraisals use: the county public record and the closed sales reported by the multiple listing service. What it deliberately does not contain is a value conclusion. It shows you the evidence and teaches you how to read it.

What you receive

  • Your property's public record — what the county carries for living area, lot size, year built, assessment and sale history, laid out plainly.
  • Market conditions — twelve months of trend charts plus a plain-English readout: what homes sold for, how fast, and which way prices were heading.
  • The comparable sales — every closed sale in your area that passed a screen against your home's own site size, living area and age. Nothing cherry-picked, nothing hidden; the full qualifying set, ranked and mapped by distance.
  • The raw price range — what those sales arithmetically indicate for a home of your square footage, labeled for exactly what it is: unadjusted data, not a value.
  • An honest complexity read — whether the sales genuinely bracket your home on size, land and age, and whether a full appraisal on your property would be routine or complex. This alone can save you from paying for the wrong product.

Delivered as a PDF by email, typically within 1–2 business days of payment. Retrospective dates (date of death, divorce, Prop 19) are fully supported — the analysis anchors to your valuation date and uses only sales that had closed by it.

The $99 credit

If the analysis convinces you a full appraisal is what you need, the entire $99 is credited: a Desktop Appraisal ($449) becomes $350, and a Standard Appraisal ($699) becomes $600. The credit does not apply to the $299 Basic Desktop, and I do not prepare mortgage appraisals — if a lender will order the appraisal, the $99 cannot be credited toward it (the analysis can still show you what that appraiser will be looking at).

This is not an appraisal. No opinion of value is developed or expressed, no one inspects your property, and no adjustments are made to the sales. It is a rigorous, honest presentation of public record and market data — the homework an appraisal is built on, at a fraction of the price.

Common questions

Is this an appraisal? No. An appraisal is a licensed appraiser's supported opinion of value, developed by inspecting, verifying and adjusting. This report deliberately does none of that — it presents the unadjusted data and shows you how to read it. It cannot be used for lending, court, tax filings or any purpose that requires an appraisal.

Why would I buy it? Because it answers the questions that come before an appraisal: what have homes like mine actually sold for, is my home easy or hard to value, and is a $449–$699 appraisal worth it for my situation. And if the answer is yes, the $99 rolls into it.

What if there aren't enough comparable sales? Then the report says so, plainly — that finding is part of what you're paying for. If fewer than three sales qualify, we refund the fee or apply it to a full appraisal instead of delivering a hollow report.
