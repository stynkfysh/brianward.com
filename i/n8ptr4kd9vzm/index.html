The three levels
The analysis behind all three is identical. I research the same public records, pull the same comparable sales from around November 2024, and apply the same methodology. What changes between the levels is how much of that work is written up for you, and whether I walk the property in person.
Basic Desktop Appraisal — $299
No site visit · streamlined format
Public records and comparable sales, delivered in a short, clean format that states the value and the sales it rests on without the full supporting narrative. Photos come from you or from online sources, and you can ask me to revise it if I have misread the condition or quality. USPAP compliant. This is the budget option, and it is a real one — the number at the bottom is the same number you would get from the other two. Where it comes up short is when the report has to travel: a CPA, an attorney, or another heir reading it cold will find less to work with.
Desktop Appraisal — $449
No site visit · full written report
A complete written appraisal report. Detailed comparable sales with adjustments explained, market conditions as of November 2024, neighborhood maps, the property's sales and listing history, and subject photos. Everything is documented, so anyone who picks it up later can follow how the value was reached. No site visit; photos come from you or from online sources, and revisions are available the same way. This is what most of my date of death clients order.
Standard Appraisal — $699
In-person interior and exterior inspection
Everything in the Desktop report, plus I visit the property, take my own photographs inside and out, and record a first-hand condition assessment.
My recommendation
The Desktop Appraisal, at $449
Two reasons, and the second one is the one that matters most in your case.
  • The value does not change with the level. With good, representative photos, all three reports reach the same conclusion. You are not buying accuracy when you move up a tier. You are buying pages.
  • Your effective date is November 8, 2024. An inspection I do this month records the house as it stands now, almost two years after the date that governs the appraisal. It cannot tell me what the kitchen looked like in November 2024. For a retrospective assignment, the single thing a Standard Appraisal adds is also the thing that has aged out of relevance — so I do not think it buys you anything here. Photographs from around the time of passing, or your own description of the condition then, get me closer to the truth than a walk-through today would.
That leaves the choice between Basic Desktop and Desktop, and it comes down to who else will read the report. If it is only for your own records, the Basic Desktop is honestly enough. If it is going to a CPA, an attorney, a title company, or other heirs — or if it may need to be explained to anyone at all — the Desktop is worth the difference, because the supporting detail is already on the page instead of in my file.

The one situation where I would push you toward the Standard is an estate being divided among several people who want the property physically walked, or a matter someone is likely to contest. If that describes yours, tell me and I will change my advice.
