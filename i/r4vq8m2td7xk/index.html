Why a desktop appraisal is credible
People often assume that an appraisal without a site visit is a lesser product. It is not, and it is worth explaining why.

What the industry calls an inspection, the Appraisal Institute more accurately calls an observation. When I walk a property I am looking at size, layout, quality of finish, condition, and what has been updated. I am not testing systems, opening panels, or getting on the roof — that is a home inspector's job. It is a visual and deliberately cursory look, which is exactly why photographs taken with a phone capture the same information. Wide shots from a corner of each room, the exterior from the street and the rear, and anything recently updated or in need of repair tell me what I would have seen standing there.

There is a second reason, and it is the one most people have never considered. The comparable sales are not inspected either. I have never been inside any of them. All I have for each sale is the listing photographs and the agent's description. When I use your photographs and your description of the subject, I am comparing like with like — photos and descriptions on both sides of the analysis. Inspecting the subject alone would actually create an asymmetry, because I would know it in more detail than any of the sales I am measuring it against.

A desktop appraisal is a full USPAP compliant appraisal. The research, the comparable sales, the adjustments, and the value conclusion are developed exactly as they would be with a site visit.
