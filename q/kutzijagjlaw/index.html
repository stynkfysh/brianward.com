Hi John,

Thank you for reaching out, and I'm sorry for your loss. My apologies for replying by text rather than a call — I'm out of town this week, and I didn't want to keep you waiting.

A date of death appraisal is a retrospective appraisal: the effective date is set to the day of passing rather than today, and everything in the report — the comparable sales, the market conditions, and the value conclusion — is researched as of that date. Date-of-death appraisals make up roughly half of my practice, and I've prepared thousands of them.

I'm familiar with PGA West. Values there turn heavily on location within the community — golf course frontage, fairway and mountain views, and proximity to the clubhouses — so I draw comparable sales from within PGA West itself wherever the data allows, rather than from La Quinta generally.

The one thing I'll need from you is the date of passing, since that single date sets the effective date of the entire appraisal.

Why I recommend the Desktop

Both options involve the same research and reach the same value conclusion. Both are USPAP compliant, use the same effective date, and include the sales comparison grid showing the comparables, the adjustments, and the final value. The difference is how much of the supporting analysis is written out on the page.

In PGA West that difference matters more than usual. The adjustments most likely to be questioned later are the location ones — golf course versus interior lot, view, and position within the community — and the Desktop shows its work on exactly those, with the map and the written reasoning behind each adjustment. The Basic Desktop reaches the same number but doesn't explain it in that detail.

Photographs

Ideally you'd send me photos of the home, particularly any from close to the date of passing — wide shots from a corner of each room work better than close-ups, along with the exterior and the view from the back. If you don't have photos from that period, a general description of the condition it was in works too. Something like “completely renovated with high quality finishes” or “dated but well maintained” tells me most of what I need, and I can fill in the rest from what's available online.

Photos can be uploaded here: www.brianward.com/upload-photos

You'll also have a chance to read the report and ask me to revise it if I've misread condition or quality anywhere.
