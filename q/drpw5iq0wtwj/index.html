Date-of-death appraisal — 9530 Vista Tercera, San Diego, CA 92129

Hi Todd,

Thank you for reaching out, and I'm sorry for your loss.

Date-of-death appraisals make up roughly half of my practice, and I've prepared thousands of them. All reports are USPAP compliant and prepared to establish the stepped-up cost basis for federal and California income tax purposes.

Losing access after closing is not a problem. I have access to the multiple listing service, and appraising the home based on the MLS listing — the listing photos and the agent's description — together with what you send me works well. An in-person visit now wouldn't show the home as it was on June 23 anyway, so a desktop appraisal is the right fit for this assignment.

The sale closing today is a real advantage. I can reference the final sale price in the report as evidence of what the market thought of the property a few months after the date of death. It's a market-tested data point that supports the retrospective value.

Your options
Basic Desktop Appraisal$299
A condensed report of around 15 pages: summary of the assignment, the sales comparison grid showing the comparables, adjustments, and final value, plus the required certification and addenda. This is the budget option.
Recommended
Desktop Appraisal$449
Everything in the Basic Desktop, plus the full written narrative: detailed property description, market conditions with charts as of the effective date, a written explanation of why each comparable was selected and how each adjustment was derived, a location map, photographs, and the public record pages. Typically 30–40 pages. This is the most popular choice for date-of-death work.

Both involve the same research and reach the same value conclusion — the difference is how much of the supporting analysis is written out on the page.

Why I recommend the Desktop

The post-death improvements and the sale price months later are exactly the points someone reviewing the basis would ask about, and the Desktop writes out how each one was handled rather than just showing the number.

The improvements after the date of death

Since the interior paint and carpet were done after the date of death, the appraisal values the home in its condition as of June 23, 2026, before that work. The MLS photos show the home after it, so I'll account for the difference both in the condition rating and when I reference the sale price.

What would help from you

The repair and improvement receipts with dates and costs, and whatever information you have regarding the current sale — I am happy to review all of it (the closing statement, any seller credits, offers, or inspection reports). I can pull the MLS listing myself.

Photographs

Ideally you'd send any photos from before the paint and carpet work. If you don't have them, a short description of the condition before the work works too — something like "original carpet, worn; walls needed paint" tells me most of what I need.
