Choosing an option

Basic Desktop works well when you mainly need the values for your own records, no one else is likely to review the reports closely, and keeping the cost down across three homes matters most. You can upgrade to the Desktop later and pay only the difference.

Desktop is what I recommend for most date-of-death work. It fits when an attorney, a trustee, or other family members will rely on the reports and should be able to follow how each value was reached; when you can share photos or a short description of each home, or there are photos of the homes online; and when the homes have been cleared out, repaired, or updated since the date of passing.

Standard is the better choice when the appraisals will be used to divide the estate among several heirs, not because it changes the value, but because a more thorough record helps everyone at a sensitive time. It also makes sense when no photos or descriptions are available, when a home has a major addition, a guest unit, or a heavy remodel worth seeing in person, or when an attorney, a court, or another party requires an interior inspection.

Why a desktop appraisal is credible

All three options involve the same research and reach the same value conclusion. The difference is how much of the supporting analysis is written out on the page, and whether I visit the homes. Every report is USPAP compliant and includes the sales comparison grid showing the comparables, the adjustments, and the final value.

The Appraisal Institute calls an appraiser's site visit an observation rather than an inspection, and that's the more accurate word. I'm looking at size, layout, quality of finish, condition, and what's been updated — not testing systems or getting on the roof. It's visual by design, which is why photos can do the same job. And the comparable sales I analyze aren't inspected either; for each one I have the listing photos and the agent's description. Using photos and a description of each home compares like with like.

A date-of-death appraisal also looks back in time. What matters is each home's condition on the date of passing, and homes are often cleared out, repaired, or updated afterward, so photos and descriptions from that time can be better evidence than a visit today.

You can read more at date-of-death.com/why-desktop.

Photographs

For the desktop options, ideally you'd send me photos of each home, particularly any from around the date of passing — wide shots from a corner of each room work better than close-ups, along with the front and back of the house. If photos aren't easy to get, a general description of the condition works too. Something like “completely renovated with high quality finishes” or “dated but well maintained” tells me most of what I need, and I can fill in the rest from what's available online.

Photos can be uploaded here: www.brianward.com/upload-photos

You'll also have a chance to read each report and ask me to revise it if I've misread condition or quality anywhere.
