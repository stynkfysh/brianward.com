Thank you for reaching out. A retrospective valuation as of July 8, 2025 is exactly what I do. Date-of-death appraisals make up roughly half of my practice, and I've prepared thousands of them.

Since you asked about a desktop appraisal, here are the two desktop options for your home. Both involve the same research, reach the same value conclusion as of July 8, 2025, and include the sales comparison grid showing the comparable sales, the adjustments, and the final value.

Your options
Recommended
Desktop Appraisal$449
The full written report: a detailed property description, a market conditions section with charts as of July 8, 2025, a written explanation of why each comparable was selected and how each adjustment was derived, a location map, photographs of the subject and the comparables, and the public record pages. Typically 30–40 pages depending on the number of photos. This is the most popular choice for date-of-death work.
Basic Desktop Appraisal$299
The Desktop without the extended written commentary: cover page, assignment summary, sales comparison grid, value conclusion, and the required certification and addenda. It shows the number and the comparables that support it. Around 15 pages. This is the budget option.
Why I recommend the Desktop

The value conclusion is the same either way. The difference is how much of the supporting analysis is written out on the page, and that written support is what a tax preparer or reviewer is most likely to want on file.
