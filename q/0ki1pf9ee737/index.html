Why a desktop appraisal is credible

Both desktop options involve the same research and reach the same value conclusion I would reach with a site visit. Both are USPAP compliant, use the same effective date, and include the sales comparison grid showing the comparables, the adjustments, and the final value.

On what an appraisal inspection actually is — the Appraisal Institute calls it an observation rather than an inspection, and that's the more accurate word. When I walk a property I'm looking at size, layout, quality of finish, condition, and what's been updated. I'm not testing systems, opening panels, or getting on the roof the way a home inspector would. It's a visual observation and it's cursory by design — which is why photos from you can do the same job.

There's a second reason it works, and it's the part most people don't think about. The comparable sales I analyze aren't inspected either — I've never been inside them. What I have for each one is the listing photos and the agent's description. So when I use your photos and your description of the home, I'm comparing like with like: photos and descriptions on both sides of the analysis. Inspecting the subject alone would actually create an asymmetry, since I'd know it in more detail than any of the sales I'm measuring it against.

If you'd ever prefer an in-person visit, that's my Standard Appraisal at $699 — but for a date-of-death value I don't think it buys you anything here.

Why I recommend the Desktop

The Desktop and Basic Desktop reach the same number. The difference is how much of the supporting analysis is written out on the page. The Desktop shows its work — why each comparable was chosen, how each adjustment was derived, and what the market was doing as of November 1 — so if the value is ever questioned later, the answer is already in the report. For an estate, that's worth the difference.

Photographs

While you're at the house through Monday, photos are the most helpful thing you can send — wide shots from a corner of each room work better than close-ups, along with the exterior and anything updated or in need of repair. If time runs short, a general description of its condition works too. Something like “completely renovated with high quality finishes” or “dated but well maintained” tells me most of what I need, and I can fill in the rest from what's available online.
